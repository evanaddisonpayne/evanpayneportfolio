# Translation notes · German (de-DE)

Notes for Evan on the German version of the site. Files: `content/de.ts`, `content/cases/de.ts`, `content/articles/you-all-sound-the-same.de.tsx`, `content/map/de.json`.

## Register

- **Sie throughout**, as briefed. Evan speaks in first person singular. I kept the edge through rhythm (short sentences, fragments, one sharp contrast per piece) rather than through informality, which is how the better Berlin agencies write for German CMOs.
- **The case for du, for the record.** German B2B tech and agency LinkedIn has moved a long way toward du, and the most opinionated voices in the category use it. For the hero and the article, du would land closer to your English. On the resume, contact page and case studies, Sie is still safer with recruiters and Heads of Talent in DAX and Mittelstand companies. My recommendation is to stay with Sie site-wide. Mixing would read as inconsistent. If you ever switch, switch everything.
- **Job titles.** Your English titles stay in English (Director of Brand & Growth Strategy, Chief of Staff, etc.). That is normal on German CVs for roles at US companies, and translating them would invent German titles you never held.

## Terminology

- **Work Tech and HR tech** become **Work-Tech** and **HR-Tech**, hyphenated in compounds ("Work-Tech-Unternehmen", "HR-Tech-Kunden"). Both are what German HR and marketing trade press actually write (personalwirtschaft, HR-Tech-Konferenzen). A German rendering ("Personalsoftware") would sound like a procurement category.
- **Kept in English** because German marketers say them this way: Messaging, Pipeline, Go-to-Market, Demand Gen, Case Study, Deck, Pitch-Deck, Framework, Content, Paid Media, Social, Upsell, Cross-Sell, Thought Leadership, Share of Voice, Win Rates, Collateral, Launch, Rebranding, Funnel, Full-Funnel, Sprint, Stakeholder, Shortlist, Hero-Headline, Tagline, Impressions, Engagement, Conversion, plus the acronyms (SEO, NPS, ARR, MQL, SQL, CAC, KPI, ABM, AEO, B2B, SaaS, CSR, LLM). Answer Engine Optimization stays English, as it is a named discipline with no German term.
- **Translated** where German has the living word: Positionierung, Marke, Markenstrategie, Wachstum, Austauschbarkeit (for "sameness"), Differenzierung, KI (never "AI" in German body copy), Handelskammer, Non-Profit.
- **"Sameness"** becomes **Austauschbarkeit** ("interchangeability"). It is the word a German strategist uses for this exact problem and it carries the commercial sting. "The Sameness Tax" becomes "Die Austauschbarkeitssteuer", a long compound but a real-sounding one, and it fits the subhead.
- **"Nationally"** (the Chamber) becomes **USA-weit** rather than "national", which a German reader would hear as "within Germany".
- **Money.** Running text and chips use "1,72 Mio. $", "10 Mio. $", "125.000 $" (period as thousands separator, space before $ and %). The **big case-study numerals** carry the figure only ("1,72", "+600", "200", "125+") and the label starts with the unit ("Mio. US-Dollar wiederkehrender Jahresumsatz ..."), so every numeral is as narrow as or narrower than the English (measured against Fraunces). On the resume results the numerals are "10 Mio." and "1,72 Mio." with the currency in the sentence right below.
- **"Moves"** in the case studies became **Züge** ("Drei Züge.", like moves in chess). "Maßnahmen" is bureaucratic and "Hebel" is on the banned list as the German "leverage".

## Idioms and jokes rewritten

| English | German | Back-translation |
|---|---|---|
| You're not under-marketing. You're under-differentiating. | Sie machen nicht zu wenig Marketing. Sie machen zu wenig Unterschied. | You don't do too little marketing. You make too little difference. (German "einen Unterschied machen" carries both senses, so the pun survives.) |
| Two tours at the same agency, from strategist to the director's chair. | Zweimal dieselbe Agentur, vom Strategen bis auf den Regiestuhl. | Twice the same agency, from strategist up to the director's chair (the film director's chair). Keeps the double meaning of "director", which suits a career that started in production. |
| A $10M program that had to keep earning its keep. | Ein 10-Mio.-$-Programm, das sich laufend rechnen musste. | A $10M program that constantly had to pay for itself. "Sich rechnen" is the idiomatic German for earning its keep. |
| A team that kept clearing it. | ein Team, das es laufend knackte | a team that kept cracking it |
| A small-city business group, asked to play on a national field. | Ein Wirtschaftsverband aus der Kleinstadt, plötzlich in der Bundesliga. | A small-town business association, suddenly in the Bundesliga. The football image is the living German equivalent. |
| Wrong out loud | Irren mit Ansage | Being wrong, announced in advance |
| I make the thing | Ich baue es selbst | I build it myself |
| playlist by committee: technically inoffensive, but nobody's turning it up | eine Playlist per Gremium ... Niemand stört sich daran, und niemand dreht sie auf. | Nobody minds it, and nobody turns it up. |
| corporate pablum | Konzern-Wortbrei | corporate word mush |
| a category table stake | Grundausstattung der Kategorie | the category's standard equipment |
| The AI Accelerant (subhead) | Der Brandbeschleuniger KI | The fire accelerant, AI. Picks up the "lit on fire" image from the opening. |
| Ran PR end to end | PR von A bis Z | PR from A to Z |
| A team and a scoreboard | Ein Team und eine Anzeigetafel | A team and a scoreboard (literal, it works) |
| Weekly (big stat) | 7 Tage / pro Performance-Sprint | 7 days per performance sprint. "Wöchentlich" is almost twice as wide as "Weekly" in the numeral font. Same fact, no new claim. |
| Took it into the room | Raus aus dem Büro | Out of the office (the moves card is about in-person events) |
| Go here (passport stamp) | unbedingt hin | absolutely go |

Headings that used a colon in English (case chapter titles "Key accounts: ...", "The comeback: ...", "Now: ...") use a period in German, to respect the no-colons-in-headings rule.

The synergy joke keeps its word: "das Wort Synergie" is the point of the line.
